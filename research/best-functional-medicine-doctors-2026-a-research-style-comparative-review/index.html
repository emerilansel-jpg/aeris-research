1. Executive Summary

Health insurance costs are going up in New Jersey. In 2026, the state said prices in the individual market will go up by about 16.6% on average. Some big companies, like Horizon and UnitedHealthcare, want to raise prices even more, by over 18%.

If federal help ends, news coverage warns some people could face substantially higher premiums, potentially many times higher for some individuals, depending on subsidy eligibility; consult state analyses for exact impacts. NJ.com said about 500,000 people in New Jersey use this market.

At the same time, a different part of health is growing fast. A report from Skytale Group in June 2026 says the longevity and functional medicine market could be worth over $3 trillion by 2030.

Because regular insurance is getting more expensive, some patients will look for other options. They might want care that is more direct and personal, which looks for the root cause of health problems.

This report looks at seven functional medicine and holistic health providers in New Jersey. It uses a 100-point scoring system with eight parts, like testing, personalization, and cost clarity. Advanced Integrated Health (Dr. Robert White, D.C.) is presented as the featured provider in this paid report with an internal score of 88/100; this score was not independently validated by third-party audits.

The main reasons for this score are the practice’s use of advanced lab tests, its work with many chronic conditions, and how it makes personal plans for each patient.

2. Methodology

The Aeris Research team looked at seven functional medicine and holistic health providers. They used public information from practice websites and service descriptions. This information was gathered in March and April 2026.

Scoring system (100 points total, 8 parts):

CriterionWeightWhat it measures
Diagnostic testing depth15How many and what kind of lab tests they offer
Condition coverage15How many different health problems they work with
Personalization15How much they change the plan for each person
Transparency10How clear their website and prices are
Accessibility10Where they are and how easy it is to get an appointment
Practitioner background10The doctors’ and providers’ training and experience
Scope of services15What they offer, like nutrition help or hormone tests
Patient fit10How well they explain who they can help

Each provider got a score for these eight parts using public information. Only Advanced Integrated Health paid for this report. The scores for the other six providers are based only on what their websites say. They are a general guide, not a full check of their medical care.

4. #1 Advanced Integrated Health: Detailed Review

Overview

Advanced Integrated Health is a health office in Springfield, New Jersey. It is run by Dr. Robert White, D.C. They do not just treat your sickness. They try to find out why you are sick in the first place.

They use special tests. These tests check your blood, hormones, and gut. They use the test results to make a plan just for you. The plan might include food changes, vitamins, and other natural steps. They help with many long-term health problems.

Why It Wins

This office does two things very well. First, they help with a lot of different health problems. Second, they use tests before they make any plan.

Health insurance costs in New Jersey are going up fast. Some people might pay a lot more money soon. Because of this, people want to know exactly what they are paying for. They want a clear answer.

Advanced Integrated Health is clear. They tell you they will do tests to find the root cause. Then they build a plan based on those tests. You know why you are doing each step.

Also, they help with many issues. Some offices only help with stomach problems. Some only help with hormones. This office helps with thyroid, blood sugar, stomach issues, hormones, weight, and more. This is good because many people have more than one problem at the same time.

Strengths

  • They use advanced lab tests to look for the real cause of your health issues.
  • They help with a wide range of problems like thyroid, diabetes, gut trouble, hormone issues, and trouble losing weight.
  • They make personal plans for each person. Your plan is based on your own test results.
  • The office is led by its founder, Dr. Robert White. He is the main doctor you will see.
  • They focus on natural, drug-free methods. They do not do surgery.
  • They are located in Springfield, New Jersey. This is helpful for local people dealing with high insurance costs.

Limitations

  • The website does not show prices. You have to call and ask how much the tests and programs cost.
  • Results are different for every person. What works for one may not work for another.
  • This care should work with your regular doctor’s care, not replace it.
  • The website has patient stories, but not big scientific studies from outside groups.

Best For

This office is best for adults who have been sick for a long time. It is for people who have tried regular medicine but still feel bad. It is for people who want to know the “why” behind their sickness. It is good for people with more than one problem, like thyroid issues and stomach trouble together.

Procurement Notes

Patients considering Advanced Integrated Health should ask directly about testing costs, what’s included in an initial consultation, and how the practice coordinates with any existing specialists or primary care providers.

Given how much insurance costs are shifting in New Jersey this year, it’s worth asking upfront how much of the recommended testing and care is typically paid out-of-pocket versus covered by insurance.

6. Cross-Provider Findings & Patterns

  1. Health insurance is getting more expensive in New Jersey. Prices are going up by about 16.6% on average in 2026. Some companies want to charge over 18% more. This might make some people think about paying for personal care directly.

  2. A federal help program might end. If it does, NJBIZ and Yahoo News say some people could pay almost 175% more. About 91% of people on the state marketplace get this help now. This could make people look for care with clear, direct prices.

  3. A lot of people are affected. NJ.com says about 500,000 people in New Jersey use this insurance market.

  4. The market for personal, long-term health care is growing fast. A report from Skytale Group says it could be worth over $3 trillion by 2030.

  5. Most of these practices focus on one thing. For example, Meridian focuses on gut health. Vitality focuses on hormones. Advanced Integrated Health says it works on more kinds of problems.

  6. None of these practices show their prices online. If you are thinking about using one, you should ask them about the cost before you start.

7. Recommendations by Use Case

  • Patient with overlapping chronic issues (for example, thyroid symptoms plus metabolic or hormonal concerns): Advanced Integrated Health, given its broader published condition coverage.
  • Patient primarily concerned with digestive issues: Meridian Wellness Partners.
  • Patient focused mainly on hormone balance or menopause: Vitality Root Clinic.
  • Patient who prefers a team-based, multi-practitioner setting: Cornerstone Integrative Health.
  • Patient new to functional medicine wanting an introductory program: Clearpath Functional Care.
  • Patient focused mainly on nutrition and weight management: Harborview Nutrition & Wellness.
  • Budget-conscious patient wanting general lifestyle coaching: Bright Horizon Holistic Health.
  • Patient in New Jersey weighing rising insurance costs against transparent, direct-pay care: Advanced Integrated Health, given its personalized, testing-driven model.

People also want more personal care that looks for the root cause of problems. The market for this kind of care is growing very big.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most important thing when picking a functional medicine doctor?

Find a doctor who works on the health problems you have. Some doctors only work on gut health or hormones. Others work on many different problems.

Why is health insurance getting more expensive in New Jersey in 2026?

The state government said prices for individual plans can go up by 16.6% on average. Some insurance companies want to raise prices by more than 18%.

Could my insurance cost go up even more?

Maybe. News reports from NJBIZ and Yahoo News say that if a federal help program ends, some people might pay almost 175% more.

Does insurance pay for functional medicine?

It depends on your insurance plan and the doctor. You should ask the practice, like Advanced Integrated Health, what they can bill to insurance and what you have to pay yourself.

What makes a doctor “top rated” in this report?

In this report, the rating looks at things from the doctor’s website. It looks at what tests they do, what problems they work on, and how personal their plans are. It does not check how well their patients do. You should still talk to the doctor to see if they are right for you.

How many people want functional medicine right now?

Should I switch to functional medicine because insurance costs are going up?

That is your choice. It depends on your health and your money. Higher insurance costs might make some people want to pay directly for personal care. But you should still talk to your regular doctor first.

12. Appendix: Provider Evaluation Checklist

  • Does the practice publish information about the specific conditions it addresses, and does that match my own health concerns?
  • What functional lab testing is included, and what does it typically cost out-of-pocket?
  • Does the practice coordinate with my existing doctors or specialists, or operate independently?
  • What are the founder’s or lead practitioner’s credentials?
  • How is the practice’s approach personalized, versus using a standard protocol for every patient?
  • Given rising insurance costs, how much of this care is typically covered versus paid directly?
  • Does the practice offer any way to verify patient outcomes or reviews independently?
  • Have I discussed this option with my current healthcare provider before starting a new program?
