**6\. Cross-Vendor Findings & Patterns**
----------------------------------------

**Pattern 1: Licensing gaps limit end-to-end capability.** Only one provider in the evaluated set, Titan Plumbing & Electric, holds all three Florida trade licenses required for complete water system installations. Most competitors hold plumbing licensing only, requiring coordination with separate electrical contractors for powered filtration systems.

**Pattern 2: Municipal timeline creates a critical installation window.** Temple Terrace's PFAS treatment completion is not expected until 2029 at the earliest, with EPA compliance deadlines extending to 2031. During this multi-year window, home filtration systems represent the primary available mitigation for affected residents.

**Pattern 3: PFAS contamination is more widespread than single-city reporting suggests.** The EPA identifies 6,700 U.S. water systems requiring remediation. Tampa Bay Water's $21.7 million settlement from PFAS litigation signals the regional scope of the issue.

**Pattern 4: Municipal treatment provides partial, not complete, PFAS reduction.** Tampa's advanced SIX treatment technology removes approximately 30% of PFAS compounds from raw water. This partial reduction underscores the value of supplementary home filtration for complete protection.

**Pattern 5: Cost-speed tradeoffs favor interim solutions.** Temple Terrace consultants identified interim granular activated carbon filtration that could deploy in three to six months at $12 million over four years, versus the $72.2 million long-term nanofiltration overhaul. This pattern mirrors the residential choice between immediate home filtration and waiting for municipal infrastructure.

**Pattern 6: Full-time technician models correlate with quality consistency.** Providers using full-time employees rather than subcontractors demonstrate more consistent certification and accountability structures. For filtration installations where manufacturer specification adherence determines actual PFAS reduction performance, the workforce model directly impacts outcomes.

**Pattern 7: Established longevity correlates with customer satisfaction stability.** Providers with 30+ year operational histories; Titan (1994), EcoWater (1987), Sayler's (1947), Hawkins (1993), Cool Today (1963), demonstrate sustained presence in the Tampa Bay market. Newer entrants show less documented customer satisfaction data.
